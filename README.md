## What local-first solves — and what it does not

**Solves, for inference:** no AI processor to contract, audit or monitor (GDPR / UK GDPR Art 28); no international transfer to assess (Chapter V; UK Chapter V as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, in force 5 February 2026); prompts and answers never leave the company's machines (the strongest form of data protection by design, Art 25); a signed receipt for every step, held by the company (accountability, Art 5(2); AI Act deployer logs for any high-risk use, Art 26(6)).

**Does not solve:** a lawful basis for each use (Art 6), transparency and individuals' rights, a DPIA where the processing is high-risk (Art 35; the ICO lists AI as innovative technology), accuracy (Art 5(1)(d) — fallfloor measured the small models at 39% end to end on support, so customer replies stay assisted), security (Art 32 — now entirely in-house), safeguards for significant automated decisions (EU Art 22; UK Arts 22A–22D). Under the EU AI Act, a company that builds an AI system for its own use is its **provider** (Art 3(3) and 3(11)): Article 50 transparency has applied since 2 August 2026, AI literacy (Art 4, as replaced by the Digital Omnibus) since 2 February 2025, and high-risk duties apply from **2 December 2027** for Annex III uses such as credit scoring (Art 113, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) — which this design keeps out of the first rollout.

Every duty on the page quotes its primary text, links it, and gives the date it was checked (29 September 2026). Counsel signs off the final position; the map shows the CTO and the DPO exactly what they are signing.

## How the pieces fit

- **No new hardware.** Copilot runs on the laptops already on the desks, and so does local-first; hardware is the same on both sides and cancels. Local-first's only extra running cost is the electricity for the AI work.
- **The floor** — the laptops the company already owns, enrolled as AI nodes (WebGPU in Chrome or Edge) running Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (Apache 2.0). Triage, screening and HR routing: seconds per item **measured** in fallfloor.
- **Heavier jobs, same machines** — KYC extraction and the staff knowledge assistant run on the same floor as queued jobs, sized from per-token rates **fitted** to the measured runs (about half a minute per KYC document, about a minute per answer). Phase 1 measures them on the bank's own documents.
- **The mesh, the desk and the receipts** — peer-to-peer WebRTC inside the LAN; a desk on an always-on machine the company already runs; at-least-once delivery with de-duplication; every step signed (Ed25519), hash-linked and replicated across nodes.
- **The gates** — deterministic output gates, and a dual-map go/no-go gate on every deployment decision.
- **One door out** — for model weights only, into a hash-pinned mirror.

## The go/no-go board

Each deployment decision is a dual-map case built from fallfloor's receipted evidence and this design's published policy thresholds: a positive map (what the evidence supports) beside a negative map (the evidence-supported ways it fails). One severe, evidence-backed failure BLOCKs; an unmeasured risk holds at PENDING. Each verdict is sealed into a self-verifying receipt; the page re-verifies them and shows a forged verdict being rejected.

Method: **Gary W. Floyd, Lumiea Systems Research Division — ThunderStruck Service LLC — "From GEP Stabilization to Dual-Map Ethical Reasoning," Derivation v0.2.** `dualmap.mjs` and `hash.mjs` are vendored verbatim from [sjgant80-hub/dual-map](https://github.com/sjgant80-hub/dual-map) @ 3ca840f, with their tests.
